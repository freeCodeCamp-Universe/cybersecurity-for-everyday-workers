Why this matters

Scenario opener

When you are unsure whether something is internal or confidential, the dangerous instinct is to assume the lower level because it feels easier. Safer practice goes in the opposite direction.

Core concept

When classification is unclear, treat data as the more sensitive level until you can confirm otherwise. Default-up is cheaper than default-down.

The cost of slightly over-protecting ambiguous data is usually small. The cost of exposing data because you guessed low can be much higher and harder to reverse.

What to do when you are unsure

  • Treat the item at the higher level temporarily.
  • Ask the data owner or the responsible team to confirm.
  • Be especially careful when combining data sources, exporting lists, or sharing externally.
  • Leave a brief note when you make a non-obvious decision.
What to do

Build a personal cheat sheet

Ambiguity is easier to manage when you already know the examples, owners, and channels to ask. A one-page reference removes hesitation from everyday decisions.

Takeaway

When unsure, default up and ask. The cost of asking is far lower than the cost of an incorrect low-sensitivity assumption.
