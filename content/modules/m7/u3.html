Why this matters

Scenario opener

Some information is not merely "sensitive by company policy." It is sensitive because law, contract, or industry rules attach obligations to it. Missing that distinction changes the consequences for everyone involved.

Core concept

Some data categories are protected by law or formal industry frameworks, not just by workplace preference. Recognizing them is part of recognizing legal and contractual duty.

You do not need to become a privacy lawyer to work safely. You do need to notice when a file contains regulated content so that the stricter handling path starts immediately.

The main categories to recognize

  • PII: information identifying a person directly or in combination.
  • PHI: identifiable health information with especially strict handling expectations.
  • PCI: payment card data governed by industry control requirements.
  • IP: trade secrets, code, internal research, and other proprietary assets. Unlike PII and PHI, IP protection is governed by contract and intellectual property law, not personal-data privacy regulations.
Important rule

Overlaps stack; the strictest rule wins

A single file can contain multiple regulated categories at once. When that happens, do not pick one and ignore the others. Treat the artifact according to the strictest applicable handling rule.

Exercise

Takeaway

Some data categories carry legal or contractual obligations on top of internal policy. Recognize them early and handle them at the strictest relevant level.
