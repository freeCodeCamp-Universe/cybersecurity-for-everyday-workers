Why this matters

Scenario opener

Every organization names classification levels differently, but most schemes still follow the same shape: broad distribution at the low end, tight control and logging at the high end. Learning the pattern is more useful than memorizing one company's labels.

Core concept

Most classification schemes share a similar structure from public to highly restricted. Learning that structure lets you transfer the skill across jobs and tools.

The exact names may vary, but the decisions behind them are usually the same: who can see this, what harm would a leak cause, and how tightly should it be controlled?

The working four-level model

Public

Intended for general release. Accuracy matters more than access control.

Internal

Routine employee-facing material whose leak would be inconvenient or embarrassing but not highly damaging.

Confidential

Limited business, customer, or personnel data where harm is meaningful if access widens.

Restricted

Severely consequential data with tight need-to-know access and stronger legal or operational stakes.

What to do

Map the shape to your organization

If your workplace uses different names, do not get stuck on the labels. Translate them into the four-level pattern so you can reason about storage, sharing, and retention consistently.

Exercise

Takeaway

Most schemes separate information by consequence, even when the names differ. Learn the shape and the rest maps more easily.
