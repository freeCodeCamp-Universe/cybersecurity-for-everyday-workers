Why this matters

Scenario opener

Most leaks are not dramatic database dumps. They are screenshots, chat messages, spreadsheet exports, meeting recordings, or test files that seemed ordinary in the moment.

Core concept

Sensitive data hides inside routine artifacts. Most exposure happens because something was sensitive but did not feel sensitive while it was being handled.

Recognition is the core skill here. Once you notice the data, the right classification and handling usually follow. The hard part is spotting it in formats that do not look dangerous.

Where sensitive data often hides

  • Support tickets, email threads, and spreadsheet exports.
  • Screenshots, photos, whiteboards, and meeting recordings.
  • Real customer data reused in test or demo environments.
  • Aggregated data that becomes sensitive when combined.
What to do

A quick test for sensitivity

Ask whether you would be comfortable if a screenshot of the artifact appeared on a public site. If the answer is no, the item is at least confidential and deserves more care than "routine work" treatment.

Takeaway

Sensitive data is often the ordinary material you handled without naming it. Recognition is most of the job.
