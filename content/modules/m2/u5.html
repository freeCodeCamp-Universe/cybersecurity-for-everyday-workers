Why this matters

Scenario opener

You click a link, realize the page looks wrong, and feel a strong urge to quietly fix it yourself before anyone notices. That delay is often where the damage grows.

Core concept

The earlier you act, the smaller the damage. Reporting fast is more valuable than reporting perfectly.

Most workers think reporting should happen after they are sure something bad happened. In practice, the opposite is safer. Security teams would rather investigate a false alarm quickly than learn about a real incident after the attacker has had an hour of free movement.

Response depends on how far you got

  1. 1
    Suspicious but untouched: do not interact further; report through the normal channel.
  2. 2
    Clicked but did not submit: report, scan if required, and stay alert for follow-up contact.
  3. 3
    Submitted credentials: change reused passwords immediately, starting with email, and tell IT.
  4. 4
    Sent money or data: escalate to management, IT, and finance immediately because time affects recovery.
Common mistake

Do not tidy the scene

Deleting the email, clearing history, or quietly "checking a few things first" often destroys useful evidence and burns response time. Report first. Let the team decide what they need preserved.

What to do

Your rule under embarrassment

If you feel embarrassed, report faster. Shame is not a signal that the incident is small. It is often the reason people wait. Good reporting culture depends on workers choosing honesty over self-protection in the moment.

Exercise

Takeaway

Report fast, even when you are not sure. The cost of a false alarm is low; the cost of a hidden incident grows with every minute.
