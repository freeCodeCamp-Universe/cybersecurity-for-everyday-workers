Why this matters

Scenario opener

Reporting is not the end of your job, but it is also not the beginning of a solo investigation. Once you report, the incident enters a process with stages and responsibilities that are larger than one person.

Core concept

Reporting hands the incident to a process. The reporter supports that process but does not run it alone.

Understanding what happens next reduces uncertainty and embarrassment. It also helps workers respond well when the team asks for specific details or containment actions.

The usual post-report stages

Acknowledgement

The team confirms receipt. Silence should prompt follow-up, not assumption.

Triage

They assess severity, scope, and what facts matter first.

Containment

They stop spread or further damage by isolating devices, ending sessions, or changing access.

Investigation and communication

The broader analysis and any required notifications happen through the formal process.

What to do

Your role after reporting

Stay reachable, answer questions factually, and follow containment instructions quickly. "I do not know" is better than a confident guess when the team is building the timeline.

Exercise

Takeaway

After reporting, support the process and stay reachable. The investigation is not your job; honest answers and quick cooperation are.
