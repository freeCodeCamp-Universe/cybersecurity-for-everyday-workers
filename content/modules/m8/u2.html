Why this matters

Scenario opener

Many incidents do not look dramatic at first. An unexpected MFA prompt, a misdirected attachment, or a strange sent email can feel small or ambiguous, even when it is exactly the kind of early signal a security team needs.

Core concept

An incident is anything suggesting information, accounts, or systems may have been exposed, accessed, or changed without authorization. The threshold is suspicion, not certainty.

The practical skill is recognizing the shapes that should trigger reporting so you do not wait for "proof" that usually arrives too late.

The main categories to notice

  • Account-related signals such as unexpected prompts, resets, or lockouts.
  • Phishing-related actions such as clicks, replies, or opened attachments.
  • Device-related issues such as theft, loss, or unusual software behavior.
  • Data-related mistakes such as wrong recipients or wrong storage locations.
  • Suspicious behavior or requests that do not fit normal process.

Exercise

Takeaway

The threshold is suspicion, not proof. If an event fits one of the common incident shapes, err toward reporting.
