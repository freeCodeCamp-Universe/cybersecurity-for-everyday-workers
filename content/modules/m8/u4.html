Why this matters

Scenario opener

The instinct to clean up a suspicious situation often destroys the very evidence responders need. Preserving the artifact usually helps more than trying to make the problem disappear.

Core concept

Preserve first, then report. Deleting, resetting, or experimenting with a suspicious artifact often removes useful evidence and costs time.

This does not mean doing nothing forever. It means stopping improvisation long enough for the response team to decide what should happen next.

What preservation looks like in practice

  • Keep suspicious emails intact long enough to forward them in a way that preserves headers.
  • Stop interacting with the artifact instead of testing it further.
  • Capture screenshots or notes about timing and visible behavior when safe to do so.
  • Follow responder instructions before taking destructive actions.

Exercise

Takeaway

Preserve, then report. Do not tidy up a potential incident before the people handling it have seen what happened.
