Why this matters

Scenario opener

An incident often has a short window where fast reporting can keep it small. After that, the same event may become a much more expensive investigation. The difference is usually minutes or hours, not days.

Core concept

Most incidents grow during the gap between the first signal and the first report. Speed of reporting, not perfection of reporting, is what limits damage.

Workers often think they should investigate first or wait until they know more. In practice, that delay is exactly what allows phishing, ransomware, and data-exposure incidents to spread.

Why the first hour matters

  • Security teams can isolate devices, disable sessions, recall messages, or block spread only after they know.
  • False alarms are cheap; hidden incidents are expensive.
  • Reporting is not the same as fixing. Your job is to start the response, not complete it alone.
What to do

Choose speed over certainty

If you are torn between "I should look into this myself" and "I should tell the team now," the safer default is to tell the team now. You can always add detail after the first report.

Exercise

Takeaway

The cheapest part of incident response is the report. Make it fast and rough instead of slow and complete.
