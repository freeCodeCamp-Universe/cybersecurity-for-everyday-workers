Why this matters

Scenario opener

Leah wants to improve all of her account security at once, but she has 30 work-related logins and limited time. If she treats every account equally, she gets overwhelmed and changes nothing. If she prioritizes by impact, she can secure the accounts that matter most this week.

Core concept

The goal is not to apply the maximum control everywhere on day one. The goal is to apply the right protection level to the accounts that create the most risk.

That means building a practical stack: unique passwords stored in a manager, MFA wherever possible, and passkeys where supported. The order matters because some accounts control the rest.

How to prioritize your accounts

  1. 1
    Email first. Email usually controls password reset flows for many other services.
  2. 2
    Money and people data next. Payroll, banking, HR, expense, and customer systems carry obvious business impact.
  3. 3
    Shared or admin accounts after that. These are often overlooked and can affect multiple people at once.
  4. 4
    Document constraints. If a system only offers SMS or blocks password managers, note it and raise it through the right IT path.
What good looks like

A practical authentication stack

  • Unique password for every account, stored in a password manager.
  • MFA enabled on every account that supports it.
  • Passkey enabled where available and manageable.
  • Healthy suspicion toward unexpected login prompts, even when the page looks familiar.
Important limit

Personal action does not replace organizational support

You control only part of the system. Sometimes the right next step is not a workaround; it is documenting the gap and asking IT or a manager to change the supported method. Good personal habits matter, but they do not replace policy, tooling, or incident response.

What to do

Your one-week plan

Use this lesson to decide what happens first, not someday. Pick the five work accounts you touch most or that would hurt most if compromised. Audit them. Then choose only three concrete upgrades for the next week so the plan is small enough to finish.

Exercise

Takeaway

Start with the accounts that can unlock or damage the most, especially email. You do not need a perfect rollout. You need a prioritized one that actually gets done.
