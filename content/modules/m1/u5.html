Why this matters

Scenario opener

Imran gets a fake login link that perfectly imitates a familiar website. If he were using a password, he could hand it over by mistake. If the account uses a passkey, the fake site cannot use it because the login method is tied to the real website.

Core concept

A passkey replaces the shared secret model of passwords with a cryptographic key pair stored on a trusted device.

In simple terms, the site keeps a public lock and your device keeps the private key that opens it. Your device proves it has the right key without sending the key itself across the internet. That means there is no password for the site to store and no password for you to type into a fake page.

Why passkeys feel different in practice

With a passkey, the login usually becomes a device prompt: unlock with your fingerprint, face, or PIN, and the device completes the proof. The experience can feel simpler than a password, but the security model underneath is much stronger because the secret never becomes a shared thing.

This is why passkeys are especially useful against credential phishing. A fake site can copy the appearance of the real one, but it cannot make your device use a passkey registered to a different domain.

What changes

Password vs passkey

Password

Shared secret. Both you and the website know it, so it can be stolen from either side or typed into a fake site.

Passkey

Private key stays on your device. The website only stores the matching public key, which is useless for logging in by itself.

Important limit

What passkeys do not solve

Passkeys are excellent against password theft and phishing, but they do not make device security irrelevant. If the device itself is badly compromised, the attacker may still gain access by other means. They also are not supported on every service yet, so this is a "use where available" upgrade, not a universal replacement today.

What to do

How to adopt passkeys safely

  • Start with one account you use often and that already supports passkeys.
  • Know which device or account ecosystem will store and sync the passkey.
  • Keep a backup sign-in or recovery method in case the device is lost.
  • Use passkeys first on high-value accounts where phishing would hurt most.

Takeaway

A passkey removes the need to remember or type a password for that account. Where support exists, it is one of the cleanest upgrades in this whole module.
