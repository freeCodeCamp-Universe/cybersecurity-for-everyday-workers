Why this matters

Scenario opener

BEC often contains no malicious link, no obvious malware, and sometimes no typo. It succeeds by manipulating process, not by attacking software.

Core concept

Business Email Compromise impersonates a trusted person or partner to redirect money, payroll, or account changes. It is a social attack on approval workflows.

Because it often comes through real or realistic channels, the strongest defense is not "spot the bad attachment." It is a rule that sensitive changes require separate verification.

What BEC usually looks like

  • Urgency and pressure to move faster than normal procedure.
  • Requests for secrecy or bypassing colleagues.
  • Changes to known payment or payroll details.
  • Lookalike domains or unexpected reply paths when the attacker is less sophisticated.
What to do

The verification step that matters

Any payment, banking-detail, or payroll-change request gets a second-channel check using a saved number or established contact path. The original email is never the verification channel.

Exercise

Takeaway

BEC is a procedural attack. The defense is a procedural rule: verify payment and access changes through a separate trusted channel.
