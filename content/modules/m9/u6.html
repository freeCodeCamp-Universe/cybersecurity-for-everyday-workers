Why this matters

Scenario opener

"Insider threat" sounds dramatic, but many insider incidents are ordinary mistakes: the wrong recipient, the wrong share, the lost device, the screenshot with too much in frame. The label is broader than people expect.

Core concept

Insider threat covers both deliberate misuse and accidental exposure by people with legitimate access. The accidental category is much larger for most organizations.

This matters because workers often hear "insider threat" and think only about malicious behavior. In practice, quick reporting of accidents is one of the most important defenses against insider-type incidents.

The main categories

Intentional

Deliberate misuse for personal gain, revenge, or coercion.

Accidental

Mistakes such as oversharing, misdirected mail, or lost devices.

Compromised

An outside attacker uses a real worker account, making the activity look internal.

What to do

Treat accidental disclosure like any other incident

Fast reporting matters here too. The difference between a contained mistake and a reportable breach often depends on how quickly the organization learns what happened.

Exercise

Takeaway

Most insider incidents are honest mistakes or compromised accounts. The practical response is still quick recognition, least privilege, and fast reporting.
