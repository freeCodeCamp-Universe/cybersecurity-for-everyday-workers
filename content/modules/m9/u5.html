Why this matters

Scenario opener

Supply chain attacks are unsettling because they arrive through things you were right to trust: a vendor, an update, a partner, or a long-installed extension. The worker did not make the original mistake, but still needs to react well.

Core concept

A supply chain attack compromises a trusted provider or partner and uses that trust to reach customers downstream.

This is one attack category where front-line vigilance has limits. Workers cannot usually inspect vendor internals. Their role is to notice unusual behavior in trusted tools and report it quickly.

What workers can realistically do

  • Notice when a trusted tool suddenly asks for new permissions or behaves unexpectedly.
  • Report unusual prompts, data flows, or vendor communications instead of assuming they are normal.
  • Keep updating software anyway; rare compromised updates do not outweigh the value of patching known flaws.

Exercise

Takeaway

Supply chain attacks come through trusted channels. The worker's job is not to predict them all, but to notice abnormal behavior and escalate quickly.
