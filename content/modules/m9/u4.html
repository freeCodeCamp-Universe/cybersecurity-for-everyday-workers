Why this matters

Scenario opener

A stolen credential is rarely the final objective. It is usually the first working key in a chain that leads to other accounts, recovery changes, and deeper access.

Core concept

Credential theft is typically the entry point, not the end state. Account takeover follows a sequence that becomes much easier when passwords are reused and MFA is missing.

This is why email is such a high-value target. Once attackers control the reset path, they can turn one stolen login into several more.

The takeover chain

  1. 1
    Obtain a working credential through breach lists, phishing, or malware.
  2. 2
    Reuse it on other accounts if the victim reused passwords.
  3. 3
    Change recovery settings and lock out the original user.
  4. 4
    Reset additional accounts and collect more data or access.
What to do

Break the chain at multiple points

Unique passwords stop reuse. MFA blocks password-only login. Alerts help catch recovery changes. Keeping sensitive material out of email limits the damage even after access is gained.

Exercise

Takeaway

A stolen credential is the start, not the finish. Good practice breaks the takeover chain at several different steps.
