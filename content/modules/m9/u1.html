Why this matters

Scenario opener

Attack patterns feel less chaotic once you see the stages repeating. Different attacks use different tools, but they often follow the same shape from scouting to impact.

Core concept

Most attacks share a common shape: reconnaissance, initial access, expansion, and impact. Knowing that shape explains why layered defenses work better than any single control.

This is useful because it ties earlier modules together. Password hygiene, phishing recognition, device hardening, access control, and reporting each interrupt different parts of the same larger process.

The four stages

Reconnaissance

Attackers gather names, roles, vendors, and workflows before they try to enter.

Initial access

They get the first foothold through phishing, stolen credentials, or exposed services.

Expansion

They move sideways to more accounts, more systems, and more privileges.

Impact

The actual harm arrives: theft, fraud, sabotage, encryption, or disruption.

Exercise

Takeaway

Attacks have stages. Defenses work by raising cost at each stage, not by magically preventing every attack at the front door.
