Why this matters

Scenario opener

A spreadsheet may look harmless until you notice hidden tabs, salary data, customer birth dates, or a health note embedded in comments. Sensitive data often appears inside ordinary work files without announcing itself.

Core concept

Different types of data carry different obligations. Treating all files the same either over-protects routine work or under-protects sensitive content.

The key skill is not memorizing every law. It is noticing which category of data you are holding so the handling rule can match the consequence if that file leaks.

What to look for inside a file

PII

Information that can identify a person, especially when combined into a record.

Financial data

Account numbers, payment details, payroll, and company financials need tighter handling.

Health data

Health information tied to a person should be treated as restricted even when it appears incidentally.

Confidential business data

Customer lists, product plans, contracts, and unreleased internal material still deserve control.

Common mistake

Sensitive content is often hidden

Metadata, comments, revision history, hidden columns, and failed redactions can expose more than the visible document suggests. Safe handling means checking the whole file, not just the obvious cells or paragraphs.

Exercise

Takeaway

Recognize sensitive data when you create, receive, or share a file. The handling rule follows from the category inside it, not from the file extension or how routine the task feels.
