Why this matters

Scenario opener

Many people still picture a coffee-shop attacker reading passwords off the air. For most mainstream HTTPS sites, that is no longer the normal risk. The real danger today is more often fake networks, fake login pages, or warnings users click past.

Core concept

With HTTPS now the default on most of the web, an attacker on the same network can usually see which sites you connect to, but not the actual page contents or form data inside a properly encrypted session.

That does not mean the network is harmless. It means the threat model changed. The question is less "Can they read my traffic?" and more "Can they steer me to the wrong place or exploit a weaker app or warning?"

What HTTPS hides and what it does not

Usually hidden

Page contents, submitted passwords, cookies, and data you send to the site.

Still visible in most configurations

The domains you connect to, the timing and size of requests, and anything sent outside proper encryption. Newer privacy-preserving DNS and TLS features are reducing domain visibility in some setups, but most networks still expose it.

What to do

Update your mental model

  • Expect mainstream sites to use HTTPS by default.
  • Stay focused on fake networks, fake portals, and browser warnings.
  • Assume insecure apps and non-HTTPS destinations still deserve special caution.

Exercise

Takeaway

Modern encryption hides what you do on a site. The practical risk now is more about whether the connection is genuine than about reading the contents in transit.
