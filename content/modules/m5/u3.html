Why this matters

Scenario opener

VPN ads often promise total online safety. In reality, a VPN is a narrow tool: useful in some cases, irrelevant in many others, and unable to fix phishing or device compromise at all.

Core concept

A VPN moves your traffic's exit point from the local network to the VPN provider. It is useful in specific situations and oversold in many others.

That means the important question is not "Should everyone always use a VPN?" It is "What problem am I trying to solve here?" If the problem is phishing, malware, or the site itself tracking you after login, a VPN is not the answer.

What a VPN does and does not do

What it does

Hides your traffic contents and local destinations from the network you are currently on, and makes sites see the VPN server's IP.

What it does not do

It does not stop phishing, remove malware, hide you from services you log into, or replace HTTPS.

Work vs consumer

Not all VPNs solve the same problem

A work VPN often exists to reach internal company systems and apply company controls. A consumer VPN mostly changes who sees your traffic between you and the internet. Those are different jobs, and one should not be confused for the other.

Exercise

Takeaway

Use a VPN when your employer requires it or when you specifically need to hide network-level traffic details from the local network. Do not mistake it for a general cure-all.
