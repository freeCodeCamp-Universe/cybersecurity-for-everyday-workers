Why this matters

Scenario opener

A worker uses a strong password, has MFA enabled, and logs in only over secure connections. Then malware lands on the laptop itself. From that moment, many of those other protections lose value because the device is where they all meet.

Core concept

Strong passwords, MFA, and encrypted connections all assume the device itself is trustworthy. If the device is compromised, every protection that runs on it is affected too.

This is why device security is a foundation topic. Your device is where credentials are typed, sessions are stored, documents are opened, and calls are displayed. If that layer fails, many other controls become easier to bypass.

Why the device is the choke point

  • Everything important passes through it: passwords, MFA approvals, documents, browser sessions, and calls.
  • Malware on the device can act as you after login, even when the login process itself was strong.
  • Physical loss, theft, and unattended access are still common, ordinary risks.
Threat models

Two common device problems

Opportunistic theft

Someone wants the hardware or whatever can be accessed quickly from it.

Targeted compromise

Someone specifically wants the data, sessions, or access the device provides.

What to do

What the rest of this module is solving

The next five units reduce the most realistic device risks in a small number of moves: keep software updated, lock the device quickly, confirm encryption, install less, avoid logging in on untrusted devices, and know what to do if a device disappears.
