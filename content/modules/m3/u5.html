Why this matters

Scenario opener

A borrowed laptop feels safe because the person lending it is trustworthy. But the security question is about the device, not the person. You cannot see malware, saved sessions, or logging tools just by knowing the owner.

Core concept

A device you do not control may have keyloggers, modified browsers, or saved sessions from prior users. Treat it as untrusted by default.

This includes public kiosks, shared workstations, hot-desk tablets, borrowed family laptops, and public charging equipment. Different environments have different risks, but the common thread is loss of control.

Where the risk comes from

Public computers

You do not know what software is installed or who used it previously.

Shared work devices

The danger is often session persistence rather than malware alone.

Borrowed devices

The trust profile belongs to the device owner, not to your work's requirements.

Public USB charging

Some ports can carry data as well as power, so convenience can create unnecessary risk.

What to do

The safe default

  • Avoid logging into work accounts on devices you do not control.
  • If you must use a shared work device, sign out explicitly from every account.
  • Prefer wall outlets, your own cable, or a data blocker instead of public USB ports.

Exercise

Takeaway

A device you do not control sees everything you do on it. Assume that by default, and avoid giving such devices access to your work life.
