Why this matters

Scenario opener

A useful-looking browser extension asks for permission to read every page you visit. A free mobile app wants access to your contacts and microphone for no clear reason. The risk is not just the download. It is the ongoing access you grant afterward.

Core concept

Every installed app and granted permission is a possible path in. The fewer and more trusted, the smaller the attack surface.

Software hygiene is not only about avoiding obviously malicious tools. It is also about pruning the unnecessary, because software you forgot about can still have permissions, stored sessions, and update problems.

Three questions before you install

  1. 1
    Is the source real? Prefer official stores, vendor sites, or your employer's catalog.
  2. 2
    Are the permissions justified? Ask whether the app truly needs the access it requests.
  3. 3
    Will I still maintain this later? Unused software becomes unmaintained clutter and risk.
Workplace example

Extensions count as software

Browser extensions can read pages, access forms, and observe browsing behavior. That makes them powerful and useful, but also risky when installed casually. A "small helper" extension can see much more of your work than many full applications can.

What to do

Adopt a pruning habit

  • Install less by default.
  • Remove apps and extensions you no longer actively use.
  • Review permissions for the tools you keep and revoke anything that does not match the purpose.

Exercise

Takeaway

Install less. Grant fewer permissions. Remove what you no longer use. Small reductions in attack surface add up.
