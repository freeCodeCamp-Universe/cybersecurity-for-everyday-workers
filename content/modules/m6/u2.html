Why this matters

Scenario opener

One-off permissions feel easier than setting up roles, but over time they create a maze nobody fully understands. That maze is hard to audit and even harder to clean up.

Core concept

Granting access by role is more accurate, easier to audit, and easier to revoke than giving individuals ad hoc permissions one at a time.

Roles turn access into a repeatable pattern tied to the job itself. Individual exceptions turn access into history, and history accumulates faster than most teams realize.

Why role-based access scales better

  • The role defines what the job needs, not what one person happened to collect over time.
  • Adding or removing a person becomes easier because the bundle is pre-defined.
  • Time-bound exceptions stand out more clearly when the normal path is role-based.
What to do

Ask for the role, not the whole system

A good request sounds like "add me to the reviewers group for this project until the review ends," not "give me access to everything Priya has." Specific, role-based requests are easier to approve and easier to remove later.

Exercise

Takeaway

Frame access by role and time. That makes it easier for everyone to grant, audit, and revoke without guesswork.
