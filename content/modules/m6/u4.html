Why this matters

Scenario opener

Access is usually granted with care and removed with forgetfulness. That is why so many access problems come from old contractors, past job roles, and systems nobody remembers to revisit.

Core concept

Access has a lifecycle. The grant is often visible and deliberate; the revoke is where many organizations quietly fail.

Thinking in lifecycle terms makes access a process instead of a one-time event. The risk is not just "who has access today," but "what triggers change tomorrow, and who is supposed to act?"

The three lifecycle stages

  1. 1
    Grant: the request, approval, and ownership need to be documented clearly.
  2. 2
    Use: the access should match the intended job, not drift into unrelated purposes.
  3. 3
    Revoke: role change, project end, or departure should actively remove the access.
Common mistake

The "temporary" grant that becomes permanent

Many excess-access problems start with a legitimate short-term need that never got a review date. Time-bound access and periodic review are what keep the lifecycle from stopping after step one.

Exercise

Takeaway

Access is granted once and forgotten many times. Most of the real risk lives in the forgetting.
