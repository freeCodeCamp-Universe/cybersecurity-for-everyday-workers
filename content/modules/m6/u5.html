Why this matters

Scenario opener

You usually know better than anyone else which systems you actually use. That makes you the cheapest and often the most accurate auditor of your own access.

Core concept

You are the most accurate auditor of your own access because you know which permissions still support real work and which ones are simply leftovers.

Organizations need formal reviews, but personal access review is a practical habit that catches obvious excess access before it becomes a problem for security or for you.

A simple self-audit method

  • List the systems and resources you can access.
  • Mark when you last used each one.
  • Flag anything unused for a long period as a candidate to remove or downgrade.
  • Record what you changed so the audit leaves a trail.
Common mistake

"I might need it later" is weak justification

It is usually cheaper to ask again when the real need returns than to keep extra access alive for months or years. The convenience benefit is small. The breach impact can be large.

Exercise

Takeaway

Once a year, or after role changes, look at what you can access and remove what no longer matches your actual work.
