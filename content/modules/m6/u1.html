Why this matters

Scenario opener

Extra access feels helpful in the moment: just give admin, give the whole folder, give every tool "in case." The problem appears later, when a stolen account or former employee can reach much more than the job actually required.

Core concept

Each person should have the minimum access needed to do their job, and no more. Extra access does not help the job itself; it only increases the damage if something goes wrong.

Least privilege is not about distrust. It is about assuming every account can someday make a mistake, be phished, or be misused, and limiting how far that problem can spread.

What least privilege changes

  • It reduces the blast radius of a compromised account.
  • It makes access reviews and offboarding easier.
  • It trains people to ask for what they need, not for the maximum possible permission.
What to do

The practical rule

Grant the smallest scope for the shortest time that still allows the work to happen. If more is needed later, that can be added deliberately instead of granted by default on day one.

Exercise

Takeaway

Default to less access for less time. Convenience fades quickly; excess permission lingers.
